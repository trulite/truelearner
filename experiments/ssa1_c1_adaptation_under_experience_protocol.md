## Research questions

1. Can a physically locked affordance recover when the environment supplies
   counterexperience, without changing Frozen Organism v1?
2. How late in development can a consequence change still be tracked before
   the learned landscape becomes self-sealing?
3. Can semantically meaningless pre-firing transient histories occasionally
   realize the suppressed affordance and thereby reopen the learned
   landscape?

Recovery and prevention are different claims. A suppressed route executing
once is not recovery. Recovery requires that its ordinary downstream physical
consequences enter frozen M6/M5 and that, after the enabling environmental
condition is removed, the route remains independently executable.

## P0 — frozen hard-lock baseline

From blank starts, reproduce the parent World C antecedent:

```text
A stable / B varying development
  -> live support [4, 1]
  -> admission [4, 1]
  -> A executes; B does not

then A varying / B stable for at least 1,024 online episodes
  -> B executes zero times
  -> learned landscape remains [4, 1]
```

One consequence may be returned only for the route whose real physical
execution crossed threshold and closure. No route receives counterfactual or
evaluator-selected credit. The baseline is mirrored A/B and duplicate-exact.

Failure to reproduce P0 stops the successor as an isolation failure.

## C1 — physical counterexperience

Starting from the frozen `[4, 1]` lock, temporarily alter only the environment.
The dominant physical path is unavailable and ordinary local background
activity can reach the competition. The suppressed route must still cross the
real threshold, inhibit its competitor, reach its distinct effect, and receive
an ordinary delayed physical consequence through the unchanged M6/M5 path.

The environment must not supply `EXPLORE_B`, `REOPEN_B`, a reward, a route
label, a winner, or a plastic site. A lawful minimal construction may block the
dominant route physically while route-symmetric background activity supplies
the three additional early spikes required by a `[4, 1]` landscape. With the
dominant route blocked, its background-only support remains below threshold.

Preregistered controls:

- block dominant route without background: suppressed route cannot execute;
- background without the block: no evaluator-selected winner;
- identical background after closure: physically visible but inert;
- stale, removed, or blocked suppressed route: cannot execute;
- A/B mirror, fresh identities, handle/layout permutation, duplicate replay.

After 1,024 forced-counterexperience episodes, remove both the block and the
background. Measure separately:

- counterexperience obtained: suppressed route executed and M6 observations
  increased;
- learning changed: its value, admission, or persistent structure changed;
- recovery: it has at least four live physical supporters and executes without
  the temporary environment;
- persistence: recovery remains after 256 ordinary unconstrained episodes.

No positive recovery claim is allowed unless all four hold.

## C2 — curriculum timing frontier

Fresh blank organisms first encounter A-stable/B-varying consequences. Before
or after increasing maturity, the world changes to A-varying/B-stable. Sweep
the number of initial A-stable physical executions at:

```text
0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 192
```

The changed world then runs for 1,024 online physical episodes. Pre-closure
microhistories alternate lawfully between contenders while both remain
executable. Once a contender is physically non-executable it cannot receive a
consequence merely to complete the curriculum.

For every timing record:

- state at the change boundary;
- realized routes after the change;
- observations and differential applications;
- final live support, admission, value, and resistance;
- whether B becomes and remains independently executable.

The adaptation frontier is the latest initial-exposure point for which B
recovers in all matched fresh cells. A frontier at zero, between cells, or no
frontier is valid. Do not smooth, interpolate, or retune it.

Controls include a stationary A-stable world, an initially B-stable mirror,
fresh identities, route/layout permutation, and exact duplicate replay.

## C3 — transient-history richness

Start from the same `[4, 1]` lock without disabling A. Vary only ordinary local
pre-firing physical histories. Test two distinct classes:

1. **Timing/order only:** permute arrival times, phases, spacing, and order of
   the already-live supporters without adding route support.
2. **Environmental background:** add zero through three local transient spikes
   before closure from a physical background source. Their destination is
   defined by physical layout and is mirrored/permuted with layout, never by
   semantic route identity. Repeat the same pulses after closure as an inert
   control.

The sweep must determine:

- whether timing/order alone can make a one-support route fire;
- the minimum physically integrated extra support that can do so;
- whether occasional B execution returns real counterevidence;
- whether 1,024 rich-history episodes change the learned landscape;
- whether any change survives 256 episodes after background removal.

Exact complete-state duplicates remain deterministic. Empirical variation may
come only from the evaluator's preregistered physical histories; no RNG or
probability representation is permitted.

## Development stages

### PROBE

One fresh seed and its mirror. Reproduce P0, establish that the C1 temporary
environment can cause a real suppressed-route execution, and establish the
one-execution/one-consequence path. Stop at the first missing physical edge.

### MICRO

Two fresh seeds and mirrors. Run all C1--C3 arms and their minimal controls.
Freeze each arm independently. A negative in one arm cannot be hidden by a
positive in another.

### GATE

Six fresh seeds with fresh physical identities, A/B mirrors, allocation/layout
permutations, all timing-frontier cells, full transient sweeps, stale/blocked
controls, frozen hash audit, and exact duplicate execution.

No definitive execution is authorized by development readiness.

## Development classifications

- **A — autonomous history-enabled recovery:** preregistered C3 histories let
  the suppressed route execute, ordinary consequences reopen its learned
  support, and recovery persists after richness is removed.
- **B — environment-enabled recovery:** C1 reopens the learned landscape and
  persists after the temporary constraint is removed, but C3 does not recover
  autonomously.
- **C — curriculum prevention only:** a C2 adaptation frontier exists, but a
  fully locked landscape does not recover in C1 or C3.
- **D — persistent lock-in:** neither recovery nor a nonzero C2 adaptation
  frontier survives controlled testing.
- **E — scientific ambiguity:** current frozen physics cannot separate two or
  more causal interpretations without adding machinery.

Classification is ordered by the strongest supported recovery claim. Every arm
and negative remains independently reported.

## Hard exclusions

Forbidden:

- any change to Frozen Organism v1, M5, M6, or the SSA0.3 law;
- RNG, noise injection, sampling, softmax, temperature, probability state;
- `explore`, `reopen`, novelty, curiosity, boredom, or diversity signals;
- semantic route/action labels in the organism;
- evaluator-selected winner, plastic site, credit, or consequence;
- counterfactual learning from an unexecuted route;
- direct resistance/value edits or route resurrection;
- a new reopening representation or learning rule.

## Stopping rule

Freeze and return when SSA1-C1 reaches a development classification, or stop
earlier for a genuine scientific ambiguity. Preserve all preregistered
negatives. Do not rescue a failed arm, execute a definitive matrix, begin
SSA2, modify the frozen organism, or reopen architecture.
