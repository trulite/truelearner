# SSA1-C1 adaptation under experience — development classification handoff

## Outcome

**Classification C — curriculum prevention only.**

Frozen Organism v1 can adapt to a changed consequence regime before its
affordance landscape self-seals, but the tested frozen organism cannot reopen
a fully locked landscape even when the environment causes the suppressed route
to execute and return extensive real counterexperience.

This result does not amend or rescue the immutable parent SSA1 Classification
C. SSA1 remains Classification C, SSA2 remains blocked, Frozen Organism v1 is
unchanged, and no definitive evidence was executed.

## Conjunctive GATE result

Six fresh blank cells passed every isolation and physical control under A/B
mirrors and allocation/layout permutations.

### P0 — hard-lock baseline

- Every cell developed the mirrored equivalent of `[4, 1]` live support.
- After the consequences changed, the dominant route realized
  `6,144/6,144` times across the six cells.
- The suppressed route realized `0/6,144` times.
- The learned landscape remained `[4, 1]` (or its mirror) after 1,024 online
  changed-regime episodes per cell.

### C1 — physical counterexperience

The temporary environment removed the dominant route's existing arrivals and
supplied three route-symmetric early background spikes. Thus the suppressed
route's one learned supporter plus three ordinary transient spikes crossed the
real threshold; the unavailable dominant route had only three and could not.

- Suppressed route realization: `6,144/6,144`.
- Each realization returned one ordinary delayed physical consequence.
- Frozen M6 observation counts increased by at least 1,024 in every cell.
- The suppressed M5 value changed, but consistently away from reopening:
  `-6 -> -9` or the mirrored-pattern equivalent `-5 -> -8`.
- Live support remained `[4, 1]` (or its mirror).
- After the temporary environment was removed, the suppressed route again
  realized zero times and did not persist independently.

This rejects the proposition that access to counterexperience alone is
sufficient for recovery in the tested mature frozen state. It does not reject
environmental recovery under every possible physical curriculum.

### C2 — curriculum timing frontier

The changed world was introduced after exactly
`0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 192` initial stable-A executions.

- Change after 0--8 initial executions: B became the persistent executable
  route in all six cells.
- Change after 16 or more initial executions: B did not recover in any cell.
- Preregistered adaptation frontier: **8 initial executions**.
- At 16 initial executions, B still physically realized `10/1,024` times per
  cell, yet the final landscape returned to A-dominant `[4, 1]`.
- At 32 initial executions, B realized `2/1,024`; at 64 and 192 it realized
  zero times.

The transition is therefore not merely the moment B becomes completely
non-executable. A partially closing landscape can admit some contradictory
experience and still reconsolidate around the early winner.

### C3 — transient-history richness

- Permuting timing, order, phase, and spacing of the already-live supporters
  produced zero suppressed-route executions from `[4, 1]`.
- Exactly three additional early physical spikes were the minimum needed to
  let the one-support route reach threshold before the dominant route.
- With those early background histories, the suppressed route realized
  `6,144/6,144` times and returned real consequences.
- Equivalent post-closure spikes were physically visible and behaviorally
  inert.
- Despite the rich pre-closure history and counterexperience, learned support
  remained locked and did not persist after background removal.

The authoritative SSA0.3 causal-window law remains exact: timing can decide
between executable contenders, but timing/order alone cannot make one learned
support equal four. Environmental activity can supply the missing physical
support, but the frozen mature learning loop still does not use the resulting
counterexperience to reopen the learned landscape.

## Narrow scientific claim

> Frozen Organism v1 has a developmentally measurable adaptation frontier.
> Before self-sealing, changed physical consequences can redirect the
> affordance landscape; after mature lock-in, even repeated real execution and
> downstream counterexperience of the suppressed route do not reopen it under
> the tested frozen credit/allocation dynamics.

This is stronger and narrower than the initial exploration-failure diagnosis:
the first barrier is not only inability to execute B. Physical experience can
be restored externally while learned reopening still fails.

## Controls and exclusions

Passed:

- hard-lock reproduction and 1,024-episode non-recovery baseline;
- physical block without background;
- background without the dominant-route block;
- early versus post-closure background;
- stale/blocked route non-execution;
- timing-only versus added-support discrimination;
- exact complete-state duplicate replay;
- fresh occurrence, route, and physical identities;
- A/B mirror and allocation/layout permutation;
- focused M8 development replay (`5/5` tests);
- focused SSA1-C1 validation (`3/3` tests).

Forbidden and absent:

- substrate or Frozen Organism v1 changes;
- new reopening state or learning rule;
- evaluator-selected winner, credit, or plastic site;
- counterfactual consequences for unexecuted routes;
- RNG, noise, probability, softmax, temperature, novelty, curiosity, or
  exploration signals;
- SSA2 or definitive execution.

Literal strict Clippy stopped only on three previously frozen warning classes:
`derivable_impls`, `manual_is_multiple_of`, and `manual_div_ceil`. Strict
Clippy passed with only those frozen-source classes allowlisted. Formatting
passed. The development binary's `--definitive` surface refused with exit 2.
