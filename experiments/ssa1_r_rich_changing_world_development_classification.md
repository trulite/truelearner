Status: **Classification C — contrast available but functionally insufficient**.

This is a separately named developmental successor. It does not amend SSA1

## Question answered

> Can a fixed, naturally varying environment provide enough ongoing physical
> and consequence contrast for Frozen Organism v1 to maintain and reorganize
> the appropriate executable affordance landscape without forced alternatives,
> paired diagnostics, or an exploration curriculum?

In the preregistered tested world: **no**.

The negative is not caused by absent counterexperience. The suppressed physical
side executed and returned ordinary consequences in every changing-world phase.
After the first landscape formed, the rich field produced exactly:

| phase length | suppressed executions | dominant executions |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 512 | 64 | 448 |
| 4,096 | 512 | 3,584 |
| 10,000 | 1,250 | 8,750 |

Nevertheless, all six fresh GATE cells retained the first phase's `[4,1]`
route landscape through the reversed second phase and the returning third
phase. Every dwell-time world tracked phases 1 and 3 only (`2/3`).

## Mechanistic interpretation

The fixed transient field successfully crosses the execution edge:

```text
suppressed route
  -> naturally receives pre-closure physical support
  -> executes
  -> returns a real downstream consequence
  -> becomes M6-eligible
```

But path-sensitive execution creates a large exposure imbalance. Once one route
has four supporters and the other has one, the old route continues to execute
seven times as often under the fixed field. Cumulative differential evidence
therefore remains aligned with the old route even after consequence stability
reverses. M5 never reallocates enough support to reverse the executable
landscape.

The equal-consequence R1 world exposes the same feedback from a blank start.
Both sides have stable, equal-strength consequence shapes, both execute, and
both become M6-eligible, yet every cell ends at `[4,1]`, not `[4,4]`. A small
physical execution asymmetry becomes self-reinforcing rather than preserving
both useful continuations.

The narrow boundary is therefore:

> Naturally occurring comparative experience is necessary but not sufficient
> in this tested environment. Frozen Organism v1 does not correct for the
> selection-generated imbalance in how often competing affordances generate
> evidence, so an early physical winner can continue to dominate cumulative
> credit even when the world's consequence relation changes.

This does not contradict SSA1-C2. C2 established that sufficiently explicit
comparative changed-world experience can reverse mature allocation. SSA1-R
establishes that ordinary counterexperience at the rates generated by this
fixed affordance field does not autonomously reproduce that reversal.
