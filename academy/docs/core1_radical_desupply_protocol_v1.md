## Nested profiles

### CORE1-A — continuous control

Exact CORE-B material law and its existing externally triggered, radius-two,
preassembled two-contact signed proposal morphology. This is the strongest
control, not the desired endpoint.

### CORE1-B — primitive structural edits

Replace the preassembled `C+`/`C-` morphology with two generic graph edits:

1. **link perturbation:** expose weak `+` and `-` direct ARROW alternatives
   between a locally active source and an ordinary nearby CELL;
2. **subdivision perturbation:** on a later opportunity, an existing direct
   ARROW may be copied through one fresh ordinary junction, preserving its
   signed material efficacy.

Subdivision is a general topology edit, not a contact/credit operation. It
knows only the source, target, and existing physical link. A junction already
reached through same-position incident topology is not recursively subdivided.
One opportunity is bounded by the finite live local neighborhood. Unsupported
edits decay and orphan junctions disappear.

CORE1-B retains external-arrival triggering and physical radius two.

### CORE1-C — ordinary-activity variation

CORE1-B, except every ordinary CELL firing can expose the same primitive
edits. Boundary origin is not consulted.

### CORE1-D — distance-graded opportunity

CORE1-C without a hard radius. Every finite frozen world preallocates enough
capacity. Candidate signed efficacy is `ONE/(1+distance)` and subdivision
preserves the candidate's actual efficacy. No post-observation neighbor,
capacity, or usefulness selection occurs.

### CORE1-E — no qualified-closure trigger

replacement. Modulation may still change material where it locally meets
participation, but it cannot invoke a categorical QLP relay. This destructive
profile asks where deep closure first fails when that affordance is absent.
