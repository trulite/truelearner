## Model

The complete law is the product of two transformations over the same connected
component partition of physical outcome sources:

1. output choice maps each component's candidates to that component's ordinary
   winning output set, then combines the disconnected sets;
2. fresh-opportunity allocation maps each component's blocked and executable
   candidates to at most its ordinary local promotion, then combines the
   disconnected promotions.

The output-only ablation applies transformation 1 with the predecessor's global
opportunity allocation. The opportunity-only ablation applies transformation 2 with
the predecessor's global output competition. The complete protocol applies both.
These are opt-in protocol values, not anatomical labels or special evaluator paths.

## Risks and rollback

The protocol enum has many cumulative predicates, so omitting one ablation variant
from an inherited rule could create a false ablation result. Exact one-module
identity and focused predicate tests expose this. A clean commit could accidentally
capture unrelated generated state; inspect the full staged set and validate all
receipts before committing. Authority could overstate generality; the protocol,
evidence, adjudication, program claim, and oracle must all repeat the narrow scope.
Rollback removes the two diagnostic variants and authority declaration while leaving
the already successful complete opt-in protocol and immutable negative evidence.
